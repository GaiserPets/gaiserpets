Rabbits, guinea pigs and hamsters each need a different base diet. Fresh foods are great extras, but some human foods are dangerous.

Rabbits & guinea pigs

  • Unlimited grass hay (timothy, orchard, oat) every day; it wears down teeth and keeps guts moving.
  • Alfalfa hay and pellets only for babies (under 6 months) or pregnant/nursing animals.
  • Measured plain pellets daily; skip mixes with seeds, corn or colored bits.

Guinea pigs need vitamin C

  • They can't make their own vitamin C; without it they get scurvy.
  • Daily vitamin C foods: bell pepper, parsley, kale, broccoli, plus pellets made for guinea pigs.
  • Don't rely on vitamin drops in the water; they lose strength fast and can make pigs drink less.

Hamsters

  • Hamster pellets or lab blocks as the base, plus tiny pieces of veggies.
  • Occasional treats: a bit of cooked plain egg, unsalted seeds, small piece of apple (no seeds).

Safe fresh foods

  • Romaine, green and red leaf lettuce, cilantro, parsley, basil, dandelion greens, bell pepper.
  • Carrot tops, broccoli and kale in small amounts.
  • Fruit is a treat: a thumbnail-size piece of apple, berry or banana a few times a week.

Toxic or unsafe

  • Chocolate, caffeine, alcohol, avocado, onion, garlic, leeks, chives, and raw beans.
  • Rhubarb, potato leaves or green potatoes, apple seeds and fruit pits.
  • Iceberg lettuce, salty or sugary snacks, bread, crackers, nuts for rabbits, yogurt drops.
  • Citrus and sticky foods for hamsters.

Introducing new foods

  • Add one new food at a time in small amounts and watch the poop for 2-3 days.
  • Not eating or no poop for 12 hours in a rabbit or guinea pig is an emergency; call a vet.

Sources: House Rabbit Society - Quick Guide to Rabbits, Merck Veterinary Manual - Housing and Nutrition of Guinea Pigs, Royal Veterinary College - Syrian Hamster Care Advice
