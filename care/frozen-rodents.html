Frozen/Thawed Rodent Feeding Safety

Frozen/thawed rodents are safer for your snake than live prey, which can bite and injure. Handle them like raw meat.

Storage

  • Keep frozen in a sealed bag or container, separate from people food.
  • Use within about 6 months; freezer-burned prey can be refused.
  • Never refreeze thawed prey.

Thawing

  • Best: thaw overnight in the refrigerator in a sealed bag.
  • Then warm the sealed bag in warm (not boiling) water for 15-30 minutes until warm all through.
  • Never microwave or boil. It cooks unevenly, can burst the prey, and leaves cold spots.
  • Prey should feel warm to the touch, about body temperature, before feeding.

Sizing

  • Pick prey about as wide as the widest part of your snake's body (or 10-15% of its weight).
  • Too big can cause regurgitation; too small leaves your snake hungry.
  • Size up gradually as your snake grows.

Feeding

  • Offer with long feeding tongs, never fingers, and wiggle gently to get attention.
  • If your snake refuses, remove the prey after a few hours and throw it away.
  • Don't handle your snake for 48 hours after a meal.

Germ safety

  • Rodents can carry Salmonella. Wash hands well after touching prey, tongs or bags.
  • Thaw and prep away from kitchen and food areas, with tools used only for feeding. Freezing does not kill Salmonella.
  • Children under 5, pregnant women and people with weak immune systems should not handle feeder rodents.

Sources: FDA - Salmonella, Feeder Rodents, and Pet Reptiles and Amphibians, ReptiFiles via Zen Habitats - Corn Snake Care Guide, PetMD - Ball Python Care Sheet
