Summer Heat Safety for Pets

Sacramento Valley summers regularly pass 100°F. Heat can hurt pets fast, so plan ahead before the next heat wave.

Cars

  • Never leave a pet in a parked car, even with windows cracked. Cars heat up in minutes.
  • It is also against California law to leave an animal in a car in dangerous heat.
  • Bring fish and reptiles straight home from the store.

Dogs & cats

  • Walk at dawn or after sunset. If pavement is too hot for your hand for 7 seconds, it's too hot for paws.
  • Always give shade and fresh water; bring pets indoors on hot afternoons.
  • Flat-faced breeds, seniors and overweight pets overheat fastest.
  • Heatstroke signs: heavy panting, drooling, weakness, vomiting, collapse. Call a vet right away.

Small animals & birds

  • Rabbits, guinea pigs, hamsters and chinchillas can die above 80°F. Keep them in AC.
  • Move cages away from sunny windows; offer a frozen water bottle wrapped in a towel.
  • Birds: keep out of direct sun and hot rooms; watch for panting and wings held out.

Reptile & amphibian enclosures

  • A tank in a sunny window or hot room can overheat in minutes.
  • Check the cool side with a thermometer; it should stay cool.
  • Use thermostats on all heat sources; you may need less heat in summer.
  • Crested geckos, frogs and many amphibians are in danger above 80-85°F.

Fish & ponds

  • Warm water holds less oxygen. Add an air stone, and keep tanks out of the sun.
  • Ponds: run aeration 24/7, shade part of the surface, and feed only in the morning.
  • Top off evaporation with dechlorinated water. Fish gasping at the surface need oxygen now.

Power outages

  • Have a plan: battery air pump, ice packs, and a cool room to move pets to.

Sources: ASPCA - Lifesaving Tips to Keep Pets Safe in Above-Average Temperatures, Merck Veterinary Manual - Housing and Nutrition of Guinea Pigs, VIN - Water Quality in Koi Practice
