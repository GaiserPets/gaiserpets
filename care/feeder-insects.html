Feeder Insects Guide

Insect-eating reptiles and amphibians · Feeding

Live insects feed most lizards and frogs. Keeping them healthy and well-fed makes them better food for your pet.

Common feeders

  • Crickets: good staple; remove uneaten ones so they don't bite your pet.
  • Dubia roaches: meaty staple, can't climb smooth walls, quiet and easy to keep.
  • Mealworms: OK in moderation; tough shells, best for adults.
  • Superworms: higher fat and can bite; for larger adult lizards only.

Treat & specialty feeders

  • Hornworms: soft and juicy, great for hydration and picky eaters.
  • Black soldier fly larvae: naturally high in calcium; good for small lizards.
  • Waxworms: very fatty; occasional treats only.

Size rule

  • Feed insects no bigger than the space between your pet's eyes.
  • Too-large insects can cause choking, impaction or paralysis in young reptiles.

Gut-loading

  • Feed insects fresh veggies or a gut-load diet for 24-48 hours before offering them.
  • Give a water source (water crystals or veggie slices) so they stay healthy.
  • Keep feeders in a ventilated bin at room temperature; remove dead insects.

Dusting schedule

  • Plain calcium (no D3) on most feedings for pets with good UVB.
  • Multivitamin about once a week; babies may need calcium every feeding. Follow your vet and the label.
  • Shake insects in a bag or cup with a pinch of powder right before feeding.

Never feed

  • Wild-caught bugs: they can carry pesticides or parasites.
  • Fireflies or other glowing insects: they are toxic to reptiles.
