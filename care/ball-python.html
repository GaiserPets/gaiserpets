Ball pythons are calm, shy snakes that curl into a ball when nervous. They are a long commitment and can live 20-30 years.

Quick facts

  • Adult size 3-5 feet; lifespan 20-30 years.
  • Docile and slow-moving; house one snake per enclosure.

Habitat

  • Adult minimum 4' x 2' x 2'; babies can start smaller and move up.
  • Moisture-holding substrate 3-4 inches deep (coconut fiber, cypress mulch or soil mix).
  • At least two snug hides, one warm and one cool, plus branches and plants for cover.
  • Secure locking lid; ball pythons are escape artists.

Heat & light

  • Warm basking spot 88-92°F; cool side 72-80°F.
  • Nights no colder than 70°F; heat on a thermostat.
  • Low UVB (T5 5-6%) on a 12-hour day is helpful; replace every 6-12 months.

Humidity & water

  • Keep humidity 60-80%. Mist and keep the lower substrate damp.
  • Water bowl big enough to soak in; change it often.

Feeding

  • Feed frozen/thawed rodents with tongs; prey no wider than the snake's widest part.
  • Babies every 5-7 days, juveniles every 7-10 days, adults every 2-4 weeks.
  • Skipping meals in winter or during shed is common; watch body weight.
  • Don't handle for 48 hours after a meal.

Handling & health

  • Support the body with both hands; short sessions at first.
  • See a reptile vet for: bubbly nose or mouth, wheezing, stuck shed, mites, weight loss, head stargazing.

Starter checklist

  • 4x2x2 enclosure, heat lamp or radiant panel, thermostat, digital thermometer/hygrometer.
  • Two hides, substrate, large water bowl, feeding tongs, frozen rodents.
