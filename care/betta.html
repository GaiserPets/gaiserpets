Bettas are colorful, smart fish with big personalities. They are tropical and need a heated, filtered tank, never a bowl or vase.

Quick facts

  • Adult size 2.5-3 inches; lifespan 3-5 years.
  • Males must live alone; they fight other bettas and may nip fancy-finned fish.

Tank setup

  • Minimum 5 gallons with a lid; bettas can jump.
  • Gentle filter; long fins struggle in strong current.
  • Silk or live plants, a cave, and a resting leaf near the surface.
  • Bettas breathe air too, so leave a little space between the water and the lid.

Water

  • Adjustable heater set to 78-80°F; check the thermometer daily.
  • Cycle the tank before adding the fish: ammonia 0, nitrite 0, nitrate under 20 ppm.
  • Change about 25% weekly with dechlorinated, temperature-matched water.

Diet & feeding

  • 2-4 betta pellets once or twice a day; only what it eats in 1-2 minutes.
  • Frozen bloodworms or brine shrimp as treats 1-2 times a week.
  • Overfeeding causes bloating and dirty water. Remove leftovers.

Tankmates

  • Optional in 10+ gallons: snails, shrimp, or small peaceful fish. Ask us first.
  • Never with another male betta, or with fin-nippers.

Health warning signs

  • Ask us or a fish vet about: clumped or ragged fins, white spots, fuzzy patches, bloating, lying on the bottom.
  • Bring a water sample in for testing at the first sign of trouble.

Starter checklist

  • 5+ gallon tank with lid, gentle filter, heater, thermometer.
  • Dechlorinator, test kit, plants and hide, betta pellets, gravel vacuum.

Sources: Chewy Education - Betta Fish Water Temperature, Practical Fishkeeping - How to Acclimatise Your New Fish, Merck Veterinary Manual - Disorders and Diseases of Fish (owner version)
