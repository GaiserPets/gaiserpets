Koi and pond goldfish can live for decades in a healthy pond. Yuba City's hot summers and cool winters call for seasonal care.

Pond basics

  • Koi need at least 1,000 gallons and 3+ feet of depth; deeper water stays cooler in summer.
  • Good biological filter plus a pump that moves the full pond volume about every 1-2 hours.
  • Cover 1/3 to 1/2 of the surface with plants or shade cloth.
  • Netting or steep sides help protect fish from herons and raccoons.

Water quality

  • Ammonia 0, nitrite 0, nitrate under 20 ppm; keep pH steady around 7-8.5.
  • Test weekly in warm months; treat all top-off water with dechlorinator.
  • Change about 10% weekly in summer to replace evaporation and dilute waste.

Feeding by water temperature

  • Above 60°F: feed 1-2 times a day, only what is eaten in 5 minutes.
  • 50-60°F: feed a small amount of easy-to-digest (wheat germ) food every few days.
  • Below about 50°F: stop feeding. Fish can't digest food and it fouls the water.
  • Above about 85°F: feed lightly in the morning only.

Summer heat (June-September)

  • Warm water holds less oxygen. Run an air pump or waterfall 24/7.
  • Gasping at the surface, especially at dawn, means low oxygen. Add aeration right away.
  • Never turn the pump off in hot weather; skim debris and top off daily if needed.

Winter & new fish

  • Keep the pump and air running all winter; clear leaves before the rains.
  • Quarantine new fish 3-4 weeks before adding them to your pond.
  • Never release pond fish into creeks or canals.

Starter checklist

  • Pond liner, pump, biological filter, UV clarifier, air pump and stones.
  • Dechlorinator, pond test kit, floating thermometer, net, quality pond food, plants.

Sources: VIN - Water Quality in Koi Practice, Clemson HGIC - Supplemental Feeding for Recreational Ponds, The Pond Guy - Overwintering Koi and Goldfish
