Land hermit crabs are social, long-lived animals that need warm, humid air, deep sand to dig in, and spare shells to move into.

Quick facts

  • With good care they can live 10-20+ years.
  • Social: keep 2 or more. They are most active at night.

Habitat

  • At least 20 gallons for 2 crabs; go bigger for more crabs.
  • Substrate: play sand mixed with coconut fiber, damp like sandcastle sand.
  • Substrate depth 6 inches, or 3 times the height of your biggest crab, so they can bury to molt.
  • Glass or solid lid to hold humidity; climbing branches, hides and fake plants.

Heat & humidity

  • Keep 75-80°F, measured near the sand.
  • Humidity 70-80%; low humidity damages their gills. Use a digital hygrometer.
  • Heat mat on the back wall (not under the sand), on a thermostat.

Water

  • Two dishes: dechlorinated fresh water and marine-salt saltwater (never table salt or untreated tap).
  • Deep enough to dip into, with a way out so small crabs don't drown.
  • Change both daily.

Diet & shells

  • Varied diet daily: crab food, fresh fruit and veggies, unsalted nuts, cooked egg, fish.
  • Remove old food daily. Offer cuttlebone for calcium. Avoid seasoned, salted or preserved foods.
  • Always offer 3+ natural, unpainted shells slightly bigger than each crab's shell.

Molting & health

  • Buried crabs are often molting. Don't dig them up; molts can take weeks.
  • See an exotic vet or ask us if a crab is out of its shell, limp, or losing legs.

Starter checklist

  • 20+ gallon glass tank with lid, sand, coconut fiber, heat mat, thermostat, digital thermometer/hygrometer.
  • Two water dishes, dechlorinator, marine salt, spare shells, hides, climbing decor, food.

Sources: Hermit Crab Association - Optimal Tank Conditions, Chewy Education - Hermit Crab Care Sheet
