A tropical community tank can hold many colorful, peaceful fish. The key is patience: let the tank cycle before adding fish, then stock slowly.

Setting up

  • Bigger is easier: 20+ gallons stays more stable than a small tank.
  • Rinse gravel and decor in plain water. Never use soap.
  • Fill with dechlorinated water, run the filter and heater (76-80°F) 24/7.
  • Place the tank away from sunny windows, AC vents and heaters.

The nitrogen cycle

  • Fish waste makes ammonia; good bacteria turn it into nitrite, then into safer nitrate.
  • These bacteria take 4-6 weeks to grow in the filter. This is called cycling.
  • Start the cycle with a small ammonia source or bottled bacteria; ask us how.
  • Cycled = ammonia 0 and nitrite 0, with nitrate showing. Test weekly.

Adding fish slowly

  • Add only 2-4 small fish at a time, then wait 1-2 weeks and re-test before adding more.
  • Check adult size, school size and temperament before you buy. Ask us about compatibility.
  • Many fish (tetras, rasboras, corydoras) need groups of 6 or more.

Feeding

  • Feed once or twice a day, only what is eaten in 1-2 minutes.
  • Mix flakes or pellets with frozen foods; bottom feeders need sinking food.
  • Overfeeding is the #1 cause of bad water.

Maintenance

  • Change 20-25% weekly with a gravel vacuum; treat new water with dechlorinator.
  • Rinse filter media in old tank water, not tap water.
  • Leave lights on 8-10 hours a day to limit algae.

Starter checklist

  • Tank, lid and light, filter, adjustable heater, thermometer.
  • Dechlorinator, liquid test kit (ammonia, nitrite, nitrate, pH), gravel vacuum, bucket for fish only.
  • Gravel, plants, hiding spots, fish food.

Sources: Practical Fishkeeping - How to Acclimatise Your New Fish, Merck Veterinary Manual - Disorders and Diseases of Fish (owner version), Chewy Education - How To Acclimate Fish the Right Way
