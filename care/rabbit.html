Rabbits are smart, social and can be litter trained. They live 8-12 years and do best as indoor family members with plenty of hay.

Quick facts

  • Lifespan 8-12+ years; size varies by breed (2-12+ lbs).
  • Spaying or neutering improves behavior and lowers cancer risk.
  • Not a good fit for very young children; rabbits are fragile and dislike being held.

Housing

  • Keep rabbits indoors. Outdoor hutches expose them to heat, predators and parasites.
  • Home base: an exercise pen at least 4' x 4', plus several hours of run time daily.
  • Solid floor, a hide box, and a large litter box with paper or wood-pellet litter.
  • Never use clumping cat litter or pine or cedar shavings. Bunny-proof cords.

Heat

  • Rabbits overheat above about 80°F. Keep them in AC in summer.
  • Signs of heat stress: fast breathing, wet nose, limpness. Cool them and call a vet.

Diet

  • Unlimited grass hay (timothy, orchard or oat) is 75-80% of the diet; alfalfa for babies only.
  • Adults: 1/8 to 1/2 cup plain pellets daily, depending on size; no seed or muesli mixes.
  • About 1-2 cups of mixed leafy greens per 5 lbs body weight daily; introduce slowly.
  • Treats: no more than 1 teaspoon of fruit a day. Fresh water always.

Handling & health

  • Never lift by the ears. Support the chest and hindquarters.
  • See a rabbit-savvy vet right away if your rabbit stops eating or pooping for 12 hours.
  • Also: drooling, head tilt, runny eyes or nose, or diarrhea.

Starter checklist

  • Exercise pen, hide box, litter box and litter, hay rack, heavy bowl, water bottle or bowl.
  • Timothy hay, plain pellets, chew toys, brush, nail clippers.

Sources: House Rabbit Society - Quick Guide to Rabbits, House Rabbit Society Chicago - Rabbit Diet
