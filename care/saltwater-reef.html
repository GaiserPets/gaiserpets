Saltwater fish · Care sheet

Saltwater tanks are rewarding but need stable water and patience. Start with hardy fish, learn to test, and add corals only after the tank is mature.

Setting up

  • Start with 30+ gallons; small saltwater tanks swing fast and are harder to keep.
  • Use RO/DI (purified) water and marine salt mix; never tap water or table salt.
  • Live or dry rock, sand, a return pump, powerheads for flow, a heater, and a protein skimmer if possible.

Water targets

  • Salinity 1.024-1.026 specific gravity; measure with a refractometer.
  • Temperature 76-80°F; pH 8.1-8.4; ammonia 0; nitrite 0.
  • Fish-only nitrate under 30 ppm. Reef: keep nitrate and phosphate low.
  • Reef: alkalinity 8-12 dKH, calcium 380-450 ppm, magnesium 1,250-1,350 ppm.

Cycling & stocking

  • Cycle 4-8 weeks until ammonia and nitrite stay at 0.
  • Add 1-2 hardy fish at a time, a few weeks apart. Quarantine new fish if possible.
  • Ask us before mixing species; many saltwater fish are territorial.

Daily & weekly care

  • Top off evaporation daily with fresh RO/DI water only. Salt doesn't evaporate.
  • Hot, dry summer air speeds evaporation; an auto top-off helps.
  • Change 10-20% every 1-2 weeks with pre-mixed, temperature-matched saltwater.
  • Feed small amounts once or twice a day; remove leftovers.

Corals & lighting

  • Wait until the tank is stable for a few months before adding corals.
  • Corals need reef lighting and steady alkalinity; test weekly.

Starter checklist

  • Tank, heater, powerheads, filter or sump, skimmer, reef or fish light.
  • Marine salt, RO/DI water, refractometer, saltwater test kit, rock, sand, buckets, thermometer.

Sources: Hanna Instruments - Saltwater Aquarium Water Parameters Guidelines, Merck Veterinary Manual - Disorders and Diseases of Fish (owner version)
