Guinea pigs are social, chatty animals that live 5-9 years. They need a buddy, lots of hay, and vitamin C every day.

Quick facts

  • Adult size 8-12 inches, 2-3 lbs; lifespan 5-9 years.
  • Social: keep at least two of the same sex (or a neutered male with females).

Habitat

  • Minimum 7.5 sq ft for one; 10+ sq ft (about 30" x 50") for two. Bigger is better.
  • Solid floor; no wire floors (they hurt feet).
  • Paper bedding or fleece liners; avoid pine and cedar shavings.
  • One hide per pig plus one extra; tunnels and chew toys.

Temperature

  • Keep at 65-75°F. Above about 80°F guinea pigs can get heatstroke.
  • In Yuba City summers keep them indoors with AC, away from windows.

Diet & vitamin C

  • Unlimited grass hay (timothy or orchard) every day; alfalfa only for babies or pregnant sows.
  • About 1/8 cup plain guinea pig pellets per pig daily.
  • About 1 cup fresh veggies daily, including vitamin C foods such as bell pepper or parsley.
  • Guinea pigs can't make vitamin C. Without it they get scurvy.

Handling & cleaning

  • Lift with one hand under the chest and one under the rump.
  • Spot-clean daily; change bedding fully once or twice a week.

Health warning signs

  • See an exotic vet for: not eating or no poop over 12 hours (urgent), weight loss, drooling.
  • Also: crusty eyes or nose, sneezing, limping, hair loss, or sore feet.

Starter checklist

  • Large pen or cage, bedding, hides, hay rack, heavy food bowl, water bottle.
  • Timothy hay, guinea pig pellets with vitamin C, chew toys, nail clippers.

Sources: Merck Veterinary Manual - Housing and Nutrition of Guinea Pigs, Seattle Animal Shelter - Guinea Pig Care Sheet, Chewy Education - Vitamin C Deficiency in Guinea Pigs
